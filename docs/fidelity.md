# Simulator-fidelity conditions

All conditions share **one** environment: the same world server, physics, compliant arm, robot path, judge, and
realistic D415 depth (the fitted noise model plus a per-episode calibration error), with SAM3 perception. Only
configuration changes (`configs/tasks/clutter_pick_{A,B,C}.yaml`, `configs/experiments/fidelity_{A,B,C}.yaml`).

| | colour (what the cameras' RGB shows) | objects (geometry, physics) | practice / dev layouts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Splat composite** (`sim/composite.py`, our compositor :8152): the empty-table Gaussian splat of the real lab (scan v6507) with the real arms cut out and MuJoCo's shadows; each real object's own Gaussians, cut from its scan, at its simulated pose; MuJoCo draws the robot arm and the unscanned insides | the twin's: primitive or procedural collision models sized from the scans and tape measurements | the real banks (train 2000/level, val 100) |
| **B** | **Plain MuJoCo** render: flat colours, no texture | as A | as A |
| **C** | plain MuJoCo | **Substitute objects** (`scripts/build_condition_c.py`, `sim/models/twin_6522_wedge_substitute.xml`). Other shapes, **the same masses, frictions, contact settings and colours**, footprints and heights within the originals' bounding boxes. The program is told their own names. | their own banks (`bin/ar layouts --object-set substitute`: train 500/level, val 100), generated by the same rules |

**The common held-out target distribution** (`heldout_target` in every fidelity experiment):
- the real objects, condition A's colour, SAM3, on the test layouts (levels 0–3 × 100);
- the same episodes for every training condition and every skill version.

`heldout_B` (the same layouts with plain colour) separates "rendering at test" from "rendering in training".

## The substitutes (C)

| original | substitute (told as) | mass |
|---|---|---|
| tape rolls (rings), red / yellow / white | solid cans of the ring's outer radius and height ("red can", ...) | 16.1 / 24.1 / 59.4 g, as before |
| black block with holes (9 boxes) | plain black box ("black box") | 82.7 g |
| coiled cable (a curved strip) | black elliptical hoop of capsules over the strip's footprint ("black hoop") | 57.1 g |
| screwdriver (red handle, thin shaft) | red-and-grey bar, the handle's cross-section all along ("red and grey bar") | 67.5 g |
| glue bottle | red-and-white cylinder bottle, same height ("red and white bottle") | 113.4 g |
| sticky-note pad | purple disc ("purple disc") | 80.9 g |

What C keeps:
- the grasp affordance of the target: graspable across, at about 47–52 mm, the same height and mass;
- clutter of similar footprints and heights;
- the same colour cue, and the same colour conflicts (red parts on distractors).

What it changes:
- shapes and names: no hole in the target, no thin shaft to slip under;
- how the objects look from above and from the side.

Dynamics are held fixed on purpose. Changes in object dynamics (friction, mass) are a separate axis, not yet run.

## What actually changes between conditions, and what does not

- **Only colour changes between A and B.** Geometry, physics, depth and judge are identical, so A–B differences
  isolate rendering, as seen by SAM3 and by any colour logic the skill has.
- **A is not full photorealism.** Its background is a static scan with rigid object Gaussians; unscanned insides
  and the robot are drawn by MuJoCo; scans of short objects sit low (aligned by 11–20 mm). The only lighting change
  is MuJoCo's shadow ratio.
- **Depth is never photoreal.** In all conditions it is MuJoCo geometry through the fitted noise model.
- **The perception model is real** (SAM3, the real robot's), so appearance matters. Example: in condition C, SAM3 found
  "red can" in the scene camera's side view but not in the wrist camera's top view, where it looks like a disc.
- **C changes the objects and their layouts together.** Its banks come from the same generator and rules, but the
  layouts are not paired with the real ones.

Results are **simulation generalization**. Nothing here is evidence of sim-to-real transfer without physical
experiments.

## First run (2026-11-18/19): one Sonnet run per condition

Experiments `fidelity_{A,B,C}.yaml`, runs `fid{A,B,C}_r1`.

**Setup:**
- Agent: Claude Sonnet 5.5.
- Two sessions per condition, with at most 120 practice episodes per run (40 per session).
- The initial skill is the same for all three conditions (`initial_skills/clutter_pick`).

**Evaluation:** all held-out scores are on the common target (`heldout_target`: real objects, splat colour, test L0–3 × 100).

| training condition | own dev (val L1–3 × 30): initial → kept | practice used | agent cost (API-equivalent) | **heldout_target /400** | L0 / L1 / L2 / L3 (/100) | s per success |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| — (initial skill) | — | — | — | 315 | 95 / 98 / 95 / 27 | 16.4 |
| A (splat, real objects) | 65 → 65 (nothing kept) | 24 / 120 | $0.37 | 315 (= initial) | 95 / 98 / 95 / 27 | 16.4 |
| B (plain colour, real objects) | 65 → 77 | 38 / 120 | $0.26 | **361** | 100 / 99 / 99 / 63 | 19.7 |
| C (plain colour, substitute objects) | 0 → 86 | 46 / 120 | $0.29 | 317 | 91 / 94 / 94 / 38 | 17.4 |

**B vs the initial skill:**
- B gains 46 episodes and loses none (McNemar p < 1e-6).
- The gain is all at level 3, where its successes take longer (32 s).

**C vs the initial skill:**
- C's changes fixed its own world: SAM3 phrase fallbacks for "can" objects seen as discs from above, plus a retry.
- On the target they make no difference: 19 episodes gained, 17 lost (p = 0.87).
- It does better at level 3 and slightly worse at levels 0–2.

**A:**
- The agent stopped early in both sessions, after 12 practice episodes each.
- Its level-3 fallbacks (`pick_seg` clearing, then drag and remove-blocker) gained nothing, and it reverted them.
- It concluded that the fingertips do not fit a tape's hole.
- The Opus sessions in the same condition did use an inside grasp in that hole (`docs/results_opus_A.md`).

**What this run does and does not show:**
- **One run per condition, with a small budget that the agent mostly left unused.** The A result reflects that
  session's search, not the condition: under condition A, Opus with 400 practice episodes reached 196–200/200
  (`docs/results_opus_A.md`, a different budget and held-out subset).
- **The most solid point:** C's large gain on its own dev did not transfer to the real objects. This is because its
  fixes were about the substitutes' appearance and names.
- **B's transfer from plain colour to splat colour worked here.** It is a single run, though.
- **Not yet measured:** a fair comparison of A, B and C needs the same agent and budget per condition (e.g. Opus with
  400 episodes) and more than one seed.
